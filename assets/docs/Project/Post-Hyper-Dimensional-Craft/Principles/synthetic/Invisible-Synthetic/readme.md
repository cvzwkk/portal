### 1. Non-Quantum (Classical)
This refers to everything we currently use in standard computing. Whether it is your smartphone, a laptop, or a massive supercomputer, they all operate on **classical physics**.

*   **How it works:** They process information using **bits**. A bit is binary; it is either a `0` or a `1`.
*   **Logic:** It follows Boolean logic (AND, OR, NOT gates). To solve a problem, a classical computer must explore paths one by one or in parallel through multiple processors, but each individual processor is still limited by the binary state.
*   **Strengths:** Excellent for daily tasks, database management, streaming video, and running standard software.
*   **Weaknesses:** Struggles with "exponential" problems—tasks where the number of possibilities grows so fast that even the fastest supercomputer would take billions of years to solve them (e.g., simulating complex molecules or breaking certain types of encryption).

---

### 2. Quantum
This refers to computers that leverage the principles of **quantum mechanics** (specifically superposition and entanglement) to process information in a fundamentally different way.

*   **How it works:** They use **qubits** (quantum bits). Thanks to *superposition*, a qubit can represent a state that is a mathematical combination of both `0` and `1` simultaneously. Through *entanglement*, qubits can be linked so that the state of one instantly influences the state of another.
*   **Logic:** Instead of checking one path at a time, a quantum computer can explore a massive "probability space" of all possible paths simultaneously.
*   **Strengths:** Solving specific, highly complex mathematical problems, simulating nature (chemistry/physics), and optimizing massive systems.
*   **Weaknesses:** Extremely fragile. They require near-absolute zero temperatures and are highly susceptible to "noise" (interference from the environment), which causes errors.

---

### 3. Post-Quantum (PQC)
This is not a type of computer, but rather a type of **cryptography (security)**. It refers to mathematical algorithms designed to be secure against an attack by a quantum computer.

*   **The Problem:** Most current encryption (like RSA or Ell!') relies on the difficulty of factoring large numbers. While a classical computer finds this hard, a powerful quantum computer using *Shor’s Algorithm* could crack it easily.
*   **The Solution:** Post-quantum cryptography uses different mathematical structures—such as **Lattice-based cryptography** or **Multivariate equations**—that are so complex that neither a classical computer nor a quantum computer can solve them efficiently.
*   **Goal:** To upgrade our current digital infrastructure (banking, government data, internet protocols) so that when a large-scale quantum computer is eventually built, our data remains safe.
