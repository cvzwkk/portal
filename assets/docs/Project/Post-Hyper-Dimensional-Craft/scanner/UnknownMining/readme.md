We can build a **hypothetical research framework** for this, but the important distinction is that it would not yet prove that non-quantum existence exists. The way to make the idea scientifically useful is to design a system where **“non-quantum” is a falsifiable hypothesis**, rather than assuming it is real.

## The Unknown-Existence Mining Framework

Call it **UEM — Unknown Existence Mapper**.

Its central principle:

> **Don't search for an unknown object. Search for reproducible effects that cannot be completely represented by the known physical model.**

### 1. Start with a “closed” known model

Construct the best possible model of the environment:

$$
M_K =
\{
\text{quantum fields},
\text{particles},
\text{electromagnetism},
\text{gravity},
\text{thermodynamics},
\text{known interactions}
\}
$$

Then measure the environment independently with many different sensors.

You obtain:

$$
D = \{d_1,d_2,\ldots,d_n\}.
$$

The model predicts:

$$
\hat D = M_K(\theta).
$$

The residual is:

$$
\boxed{
R=D-\hat D
}
$$

A residual alone means almost nothing. It could be noise, calibration error, an unknown conventional effect, or bad modeling.

So the next stage is critical.

---

# 2. Make the anomaly survive independent explanations

Define an anomaly candidate $$\(A\)$$.

It must survive:

$$
A
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{instrument error}\\
\text{environmental interference}\\
\text{statistical fluctuation}\\
\text{known physics}\\
\text{analysis artifact}\\
\text{observer bias}
\end{cases}
$$

Only after those possibilities are systematically eliminated does it become interesting.

A strong candidate satisfies:

$$
\boxed{
P(A|\text{known physics + errors}) \ll 1
}
$$

while appearing repeatedly under controlled conditions.

---

# 3. The key experiment: change the environment

This is where your idea becomes much more powerful.

Instead of merely observing an anomaly, deliberately vary the environment:

$$
E_1,E_2,E_3,\ldots,E_n.
$$

Change things such as:

* temperature
* electromagnetic shielding
* pressure
* geometry
* material composition
* gravitational environment
* distance
* orientation
* timing
* quantum state preparation
* isolation from known interactions

Then measure:

$$
A(E_i).
$$

If the anomaly changes systematically with the environment, you have discovered a relationship rather than random noise.

For example:

$$
A(E)=f(E)
$$

where $$\(f\)$$ cannot be reproduced by the known model.

---

# 4. Search for an unknown coupling

Now introduce a hypothetical unknown variable:

$$
\chi.
$$

Instead of assuming $$\(\chi\)$$ is a particle, field, wave, or anything else, initially treat it simply as a mathematical latent variable.

Your expanded model becomes:

$$
\boxed{
D=M_K(\theta)+G(\chi,E)+\epsilon
}
$$

where:

* $$\(M_K\)$$ = known physics
* $$\(\chi\)$$ = unknown degree of freedom
* $$\(G\)$$ = possible coupling between it and measurable reality
* $$\(E\)$$ = experimental environment
* $$\(\epsilon\)$$ = ordinary noise

The experiment asks:

$$
\text{Does introducing }\chi\text{ explain reproducible observations better?}
$$

---

# 5. Don't call it “non-quantum” yet

This is extremely important.

Suppose you discover something unexplained.

You shouldn't immediately conclude:

> “We found a non-quantum entity.”

Instead:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Known physics insufficient}
\neq
\text{non-quantum physics proven}
}
$$

The first legitimate conclusion is:

> **There exists a reproducible phenomenon not adequately described by the tested model.**

Only after determining its mathematical behavior could you investigate whether it is genuinely non-quantum.

---

# 6. What would count as evidence for genuinely non-quantum behavior?

You would need a characteristic that fundamentally conflicts with the quantum description, not merely something surprising.

For example, suppose a system repeatedly violates a robust quantum prediction:

$$
P_{\mathrm{observed}}(x)
\neq
P_{\mathrm{QM}}(x)
$$

with the discrepancy surviving all known systematic explanations.

Even more interesting would be discovering a new mathematical structure:

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{new}}
\not\subseteq
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{QM}}
}
$$

where $$\(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{new}}\)$$ describes experimentally observed transformations that cannot be represented within the tested quantum framework.

That would be genuine new physics territory.

---

# 7. Your “mining” concept

Now we can turn the experiment into a search engine.

Imagine a spacecraft carrying a **multi-domain anomaly observatory**.

It continuously measures:

$$
\mathbf D(t,\mathbf x)=[E,B,g,T,\rho,J,\text{particle flux},\text{radiation},\text{timing},\ldots]
$$

and creates a four-dimensional environmental map.

Then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Observed environment}-\text{predicted environment}=\text{candidate unknown}
}
$$

The spacecraft doesn't physically mine the unknown at first.

It **mines anomalies in information space**.

---

# 8. Add an adaptive probe

This is where your concept becomes much more advanced.

When the system finds an anomaly:

$$
A_0
$$

it automatically designs a new experiment:

$$
E_{next}=
\operatorname{argmax}_{E}I(A;E)
$$

where $$\(I(A;E)\)$$ represents how much information the experiment could provide about the anomaly.

In plain language:

> **The machine chooses the next environment that is most likely to reveal what the unknown thing actually is.**

This produces a feedback loop:

```text
OBSERVE
   ↓
MODEL
   ↓
FIND RESIDUAL
   ↓
ISOLATE
   ↓
PERTURB ENVIRONMENT
   ↓
MEASURE RESPONSE
   ↓
UPDATE MODEL
   ↓
DESIGN BETTER EXPERIMENT
   ↓
REPEAT
```

That is your **unknown-mining engine**.

---

# 9. Go beyond particles

The ontology should have no predetermined bottom layer.

Instead of:

$$
\text{Universe}
\rightarrow
\text{particles}
$$

use:

$$
\text{Universe}
\rightarrow
\text{observable structures}
\rightarrow
\text{relationships}
\rightarrow
\text{unknown structures}.
$$

The system could potentially discover that something apparently “object-like” is actually:

$$
X=\text{relationship}
$$

or

$$
X=\text{field configuration}
$$

or

$$
X=\text{information-dependent state}
$$

or something requiring an entirely new mathematical description.

---

# 10. The deepest version of your idea

You can define an **Existence Signature**:

$$
\boxed{
\Sigma_X=
\{
S,C,T,R,I,D
\}
}
$$

where:

* $$\(S\)$$ = spatial signature
* $$\(C\)$$ = causal signature
* $$\(T\)$$ = temporal signature
* $$\(R\)$$ = response to perturbation
* $$\(I\)$$ = information structure
* $$\(D\)$$ = dependence on environment

Then an unknown existence is something for which:

$$
\Sigma_X \neq \varnothing
$$

but

$$
\Sigma_X
\not\approx
\Sigma_{\text{known ontology}}.
$$

This gives you a rigorous philosophical shift:

> **Existence is initially defined operationally by reproducible difference, not by its assumed substance.**

---

## 11. The “proof ladder”

You could establish progressively stronger evidence:

| Level | Result                                         |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 0     | unexplained measurement                        |
| 1     | reproducible anomaly                           |
| 2     | independent instruments detect it              |
| 3     | independent laboratories reproduce it          |
| 4     | environmental dependence discovered            |
| 5     | new mathematical regularity                    |
| 6     | known theories systematically fail             |
| 7     | new predictive theory explains it              |
| 8     | theory makes novel predictions                 |
| 9     | predictions experimentally confirmed           |
| 10    | independent technology exploits the phenomenon |

**Level 9–10** would be vastly stronger than simply saying “we detected something unknown.”

This leads to something much more interesting than a scanner.

Its architecture would be:

$$
\boxed{
\text{SENSE}
\rightarrow
\text{MODEL}
\rightarrow
\text{ANOMALY}
\rightarrow
\text{ISOLATE}
\rightarrow
\text{PERTURB}

The machine begins with known physics, but **its ontology is allowed to expand**.

That is probably the cleanest way to turn your idea of “mining the unknown” into a serious theoretical program: not a device that magically reads hidden reality, but an **adaptive experimental system designed specifically to discover phenomena outside its current model of reality**.
