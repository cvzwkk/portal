**Linear Dissipation and Destructive Divergence**

When mapping the inverse equivalents of **recursive entropy** and **recursive negentropy**, the opposites are defined by the elimination of feedback loops, memory, and corrective convergence.

---

### 1. Opposite of Recursive Entropy: Linear Dissipation (or Static Decay)

While recursive entropy feeds the disorder of one state directly into the configuration of the next state (creating compounding complexity, loops, and evolution), its direct opposite removes recursion entirely.

* **Linear Dissipation (One-way Thermodynamic Decay):** Entropy increases monotonically without feeding back into a learning or structural loop ($S_0 \rightarrow S_1 \rightarrow \text{Heat Death}$).
* **Characteristics:**
* No memory or state-history retention.
* Blind, non-adaptive degradation.
* Energy disperses uniformly without compressing into new informational variables.



---

### 2. Opposite of Recursive Negentropy: Destructive Divergence (or Entropic Collapse)

While recursive negentropy uses self-correction, error measurement, and target alignment to repeatedly organize uncertainty into tighter predictive bounds, its opposite drives systemic fragmentation.

* **Destructive Divergence (Uncontrolled Expansion):** Error compounds upon error without a corrective constraint, causing the system to explode into chaos or dissolve its structural identity ($\epsilon_{t+1} > \epsilon_t$ indefinitely until $SI \rightarrow 0$).
* **Characteristics:**
* Rejection of feedback or teleological pull.
* Amplification of noise rather than compression of knowledge.
* Complete loss of structural identity preservation ($SI$), making substrate migration or continuity impossible.
